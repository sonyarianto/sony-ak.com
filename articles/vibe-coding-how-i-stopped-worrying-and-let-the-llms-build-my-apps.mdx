<script async="" src="https://platform.twitter.com/widgets.js" charset="utf-8"></script></figure><p>There’s a new kind of programming emerging — and no one really has a name for it yet.<br>I call it <strong>"Vibe Coding."</strong></p><p>It’s fast, messy, surprisingly effective, and only possible because <strong>LLMs (Large Language Models)</strong> have gotten <em>too good</em> at writing code. I'm not really coding anymore in the traditional sense. I'm <strong>seeing stuff</strong>, <strong>saying stuff</strong>, <strong>running stuff</strong>, and <strong>copy-pasting stuff</strong>. Somehow, it mostly works.</p><p>Let me explain.</p><hr><h2 id="what-is-vibe-coding"><strong>What Is Vibe Coding?</strong></h2><p><strong>Vibe coding</strong> is when you <strong>surrender control</strong>.<br>You <strong>speak intentions</strong>, not code. You <strong>accept whatever comes back</strong>. You <strong>build apps</strong> without really "building" them in the classic sense.</p><p>Here’s what it looks like in practice:</p><ul><li>I barely touch my keyboard. I use <strong>voice input</strong> with tools like <strong>SuperWhisper</strong> to talk directly to my IDE (hello, <strong>Cursor Composer + Sonnet</strong>).</li><li>I say things like "<strong>decrease the sidebar padding by half</strong>" instead of figuring out where the CSS is.</li><li>When I get an error? I just <strong>copy-paste the whole error message</strong> into the LLM and say nothing else. Usually, it fixes itself.</li><li>I <strong>accept all diffs</strong> without reading them.</li><li>If something is broken and the model can’t fix it immediately, I just <strong>ask for random changes</strong> until it somehow goes away.</li></ul><p>There’s no big plan.<br>There’s no tight architectural control.<br>There’s no careful code review.</p><p>It’s pure vibes.</p><hr><h2 id="why-it-works-at-least-sometimes"><strong>Why It Works (At Least, Sometimes)</strong></h2><ul><li><strong>LLMs are ridiculously competent</strong> at 80% of development tasks now. Styling tweaks, boilerplate generation, simple bug fixes — they can do it <strong>faster than a human ever could manually</strong>.</li><li><strong>Conversational interfaces</strong> are now so fast and natural that the friction between <strong>idea</strong> and <strong>implementation</strong> is nearly gone.</li><li><strong>Weekend projects, prototypes, or throwaway apps</strong> don't require perfection. They just need to <em>exist</em>.</li></ul><p>When the stakes are low, <strong>speed beats discipline</strong>.</p><hr><h2 id="the-principles-of-vibe-coding"><strong>The Principles of Vibe Coding</strong></h2><p>I didn't set out to invent a new workflow, but looking back, here are some of the <strong>core principles</strong> I've stumbled into:</p>

<hr><h2 id="but-is-it-dangerous"><strong>But... Is It Dangerous?</strong></h2><p><strong>Absolutely.</strong></p><p>Let’s be clear: <strong>Vibe coding is not for production systems</strong>.<br>If you're building something critical — anything involving <strong>money, healthcare, security, or long-term maintenance</strong> — you cannot afford the chaos.</p><p>Some of the real risks include:</p><ul><li><strong>Hidden technical debt</strong>: Bad patterns accumulate invisibly until they explode.</li><li><strong>Security vulnerabilities</strong>: Blindly accepting code could lead to serious exploits.</li><li><strong>Unmaintainable code</strong>: You might end up with a codebase that no human (including yourself) fully understands.</li><li><strong>Performance problems</strong>: Quick fixes often create slow, bloated systems under real load.</li></ul><p><strong>Vibe coding</strong> is best seen as <strong>phase one</strong>: an incredibly fast way to get something real into existence.<br>But if a project shows signs of growing up, you <strong>must graduate</strong> to a more traditional engineering mindset: structured reviews, intentional architecture, refactoring, testing.</p><p>Think of vibe coding like <strong>building sandcastles</strong> — wonderful, creative, temporary.<br>Not like <strong>laying the foundation for a skyscraper</strong>.</p><hr><h2 id="a-glimpse-into-the-future"><strong>A Glimpse Into the Future</strong></h2><p>Honestly, what’s happening feels bigger than just "lazy coding."</p><p><strong>Vibe coding is a preview of post-code development.</strong><br>In the near future, most developers (and even non-developers) will interact with software creation <strong>at the level of <em>feelings</em>, <em>intentions</em>, and <em>conversations</em></strong>.</p><p>Imagine:</p><ul><li>"<strong>Make this page more modern.</strong>"</li><li>"<strong>Optimize the backend for more users.</strong>"</li><li>"<strong>Fix the security holes.</strong>"</li></ul><p>And your agent(s) just... <strong>do it</strong>.</p><p>We are moving toward a world where <strong>code is not hand-written</strong>, but <strong>grown</strong>, <strong>cultivated</strong>, and <strong>steered</strong> by intent.<br>Where <strong>humans direct</strong>, and <strong>machines execute</strong>.</p><hr><h2 id="finally"><strong>Finally</strong></h2><p><strong>Vibe coding feels like magic</strong> — until it doesn't.<br>It’s freeing, powerful, and shockingly productive — but it’s also chaotic, brittle, and a little dangerous.</p><p>Use it <strong>when you can afford to</strong>, and <strong>know when to switch gears</strong>.</p><p>For me, it’s been an exhilarating ride:<br>Seeing an idea, saying it out loud, watching it happen...<br>without ever feeling like I "wrote" code at all.</p><hr><p><em>If you’re feeling brave this weekend, try it. Stop coding. Start vibing.</em><br>Let’s see where the future takes us.</p><p>🚀</p><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
