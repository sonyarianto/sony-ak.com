<p>For the last decade, <strong>Software as a Service (SaaS)</strong> has been the default answer to almost every business problem. Need CRM? Buy SaaS. Need collaboration? Buy SaaS. Need analytics? Buy SaaS.</p><p>It worked — until it didn’t.</p><p>The uncomfortable truth is that <strong>most SaaS products are built for the lowest common denominator</strong>. They solve generic problems well, but they are terrible at adapting to how your organization actually works. You end up bending your processes to fit the software, instead of the software working for you.</p><p>That is why I believe <strong>Creation as a Service will eventually beat Software as a Service.</strong></p><p>Not because SaaS is useless — but because <strong>static software cannot keep up with dynamic organizations</strong>.</p><hr><h2 id="the-real-problem-with-saas-it-freezes-your-process-in-time"><strong>The Real Problem With SaaS: It Freezes Your Process in Time</strong></h2><p>SaaS products encode decisions:</p><ul><li>how workflows should look</li><li>how data should flow</li><li>what is “normal usage”</li></ul><p>This is fine when your business is simple. It breaks when:</p><ul><li>your workflows evolve fast</li><li>your internal logic is unique</li><li>your organization differentiates on <em>process</em>, not just output</li></ul><p>Most SaaS vendors sell you <strong>their idea of how your company should work</strong>. Over time, you either:</p><ul><li>accept the friction, or</li><li>build painful workarounds, or</li><li>leave.</li></ul><p>In the age of AI, this friction becomes unacceptable.<br>If software can be created and modified cheaply, <strong>why should my business be constrained by someone else’s assumptions?</strong></p><hr><h2 id="creation-as-a-service-platforms-over-products"><strong>Creation as a Service: Platforms Over Products</strong></h2><p>What I want is not more features.<br>I want <strong>infrastructure, primitives, and platforms</strong>.</p><p>Creation as a Service means:</p><ul><li>the product gives you <strong>building blocks</strong>, not finished opinions</li><li>you define workflows</li><li>you encode your own logic</li><li>you extend the system instead of fighting it</li></ul><p>The value is not in the UI screens.<br>The value is in:</p><ul><li>extensibility</li><li>composability</li><li>ownership of behavior</li></ul><p>This is the difference between:</p><ul><li><strong>using software</strong></li><li>and <strong>owning how software behaves inside your company</strong></li></ul><p>AI is the accelerant here. It collapses the cost of customization. Suddenly, building internal workflows and agents is cheaper than forcing your team to adapt to rigid SaaS UX.</p><hr><h2 id="why-slack-is-the-perfect-in-between"><strong>Why Slack Is the Perfect In-Between</strong></h2><p>I have zero interest in building my own Slack.<br>And anyone who thinks rebuilding Slack is “easy” has never shipped <strong>infrastructure-grade software</strong>.</p><p>Slack works because it sits perfectly between:</p><ul><li><strong>finished product</strong></li><li>and <strong>programmable platform</strong></li></ul><p>You get:</p><ul><li>identity</li><li>real-time messaging</li><li>reliability</li><li>permissions</li><li>scale</li></ul><p>And on top of that:</p><ul><li>bots</li><li>events</li><li>APIs</li><li>workflows</li><li>agents</li></ul><p>Slack becomes a <strong>control plane for how work flows inside a company</strong>.</p><p>You do not rebuild communication.<br>You extend it.</p><p>That is the sweet spot:</p><blockquote><strong>Buy commodity complexity. Build competitive advantage.</strong></blockquote><hr><h2 id="slack-based-agents-are-the-real-operating-system"><strong>Slack-Based Agents Are the Real Operating System</strong></h2><p>When your company runs on Slack-based “@agents”, something fundamental changes.</p><p>Slack stops being “chat”.<br>It becomes:</p><ul><li>a <strong>human + AI interface layer</strong></li><li>a <strong>workflow trigger surface</strong></li><li>a <strong>decision hub</strong></li><li>a <strong>control plane for execution</strong></li></ul><p>At that point:</p><ul><li>tools are no longer destinations</li><li>they are <strong>capabilities invoked from where work already happens</strong></li></ul><p>This is not “AI features in SaaS”.<br>This is <strong>software becoming programmable labor</strong>.</p><p>That distinction matters.</p><hr><h2 id="why-most-saas-is-in-trouble"><strong>Why Most SaaS Is in Trouble</strong></h2><p>Many SaaS companies:</p><ul><li>lock workflows into UI flows</li><li>monetize per seat</li><li>resist deep extensibility (because it breaks their product vision)</li></ul><p>AI changes the economics:</p><ul><li>building custom logic is now cheap</li><li>internal platforms become viable</li><li>rigid SaaS becomes a bottleneck</li></ul><p>The future is not:</p><blockquote>SaaS + a few AI buttons</blockquote><p>The future is:</p><blockquote><strong>Infrastructure + primitives + workflows + agents</strong></blockquote><p>If your product cannot be <strong>extended deeply</strong>, it will eventually be replaced by something that can.</p><hr><h2 id="salesforce-slack-a-once-in-a-generation-opportunity"><strong>Salesforce + Slack: A Once-in-a-Generation Opportunity</strong></h2><p>Salesforce has always struggled with:</p><ul><li>UX complexity</li><li>configuration fatigue</li><li>heavy interfaces</li></ul><p>Slack solves the human interface problem.</p><p>If Salesforce is smart, Slack becomes:</p><ul><li>the <strong>front door</strong></li><li>the <strong>daily interface</strong></li><li>the <strong>agent control plane</strong></li></ul><p>And Salesforce becomes:</p><ul><li>the <strong>system of record</strong></li><li>the <strong>policy layer</strong></li><li>the <strong>data engine</strong></li></ul><p>That is not a feature upgrade.<br>That is a <strong>platform re-architecture</strong>.</p><p>If Salesforce fails to do this, someone else will turn Slack into the control plane for business systems — and Salesforce becomes a backend provider in someone else’s ecosystem.</p><hr><h2 id="what-people-often-miss-important-considerations"><strong>What People Often Miss (Important Considerations)</strong></h2><h3 id="1-governance-becomes-product-not-compliance"><strong>1. Governance Becomes Product, Not Compliance</strong></h3><p>When everything is programmable:</p><ul><li><strong>permissions</strong></li><li><strong>audit logs</strong></li><li><strong>blast radius</strong></li><li><strong>approval flows</strong></li></ul><p>must be first-class primitives.<br>Without governance, Creation as a Service becomes chaos.</p><hr><h3 id="2-ux-for-builders-is-the-new-moat"><strong>2. UX for Builders Is the New Moat</strong></h3><p>Platforms fail when:</p><ul><li>extension is painful</li><li>docs are unclear</li><li>developer experience is hostile</li></ul><p>If building agents and workflows feels harder than tolerating bad SaaS UX, people will go back to SaaS.</p><hr><h3 id="3-cost-control-becomes-strategic"><strong>3. Cost Control Becomes Strategic</strong></h3><p>AI-powered workflows introduce:</p><ul><li>hidden compute cost</li><li>token spend</li><li>runaway automation</li></ul><p>Your platform must expose:</p><ul><li>cost observability</li><li>quotas</li><li>budget controls</li></ul><p>Otherwise, finance will kill your platform adoption.</p><hr><h3 id="4-security-boundaries-must-be-designed-not-bolted-on"><strong>4. Security Boundaries Must Be Designed, Not Bolted On</strong></h3><p>Agents can:</p><ul><li>read data</li><li>trigger actions</li><li>call external systems</li></ul><p>Without strong boundaries, you create:</p><ul><li>massive breach surfaces</li><li>compliance nightmares</li></ul><p>Security is not an afterthought in a programmable company.<br>It is part of the product.</p><hr><h3 id="5-platform-lock-in-replaces-vendor-lock-in"><strong>5. Platform Lock-In Replaces Vendor Lock-In</strong></h3><p>The risk shifts:</p><ul><li>from being locked into SaaS</li><li>to being locked into <strong>platform architecture</strong></li></ul><p>If your internal workflows depend on one platform’s primitives, switching becomes hard.</p><p>The trade-off is worth it — but it must be acknowledged.</p><hr><h2 id="the-new-winning-model"><strong>The New Winning Model</strong></h2><p>The future winners will not sell workflows.<br>They will sell <strong>the ability to create workflows</strong>.</p><p>They will not sell features.<br>They will sell <strong>capabilities</strong>.</p><p>They will not ship opinions.<br>They will ship <strong>infrastructure with product-grade UX</strong>.</p><p>That is the real evolution:</p><blockquote>From Software as a Service<br>to <strong>Creation as a Service</strong></blockquote><p>SaaS is not dead.<br>But <strong>rigid SaaS is already obsolete.</strong></p><p>The future belongs to platforms that let software finally work for people — instead of people working around software.</p><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
