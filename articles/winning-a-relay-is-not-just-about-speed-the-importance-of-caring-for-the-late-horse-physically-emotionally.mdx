<h3 id="introduction"><strong>Introduction</strong></h3><p>Many people believe that a relay race is won solely by having the <strong>fastest runners</strong>. In reality, victory comes from <strong>a team that works in perfect sync</strong>, especially during the <strong>baton handover</strong>. The same principle applies in the workplace: you don’t always win by “firing the slowest runner,” but by <strong>building a team that supports and strengthens each other</strong>.</p><hr><h3 id="what-the-%E2%80%9Clate-horse%E2%80%9D-means-in-a-team"><strong>What the “Late Horse” Means in a Team</strong></h3><p>The term <em>late horse</em> refers to team members whose performance is not yet as fast or sharp as the rest. This can happen for various reasons:</p><ul><li><strong>Physically (skills &amp; output):</strong> lacking experience, unfamiliar with certain tools, or slow to adapt to changes.</li><li><strong>Emotionally (mental &amp; motivation):</strong> low self-confidence, fear of making mistakes, or feeling like a burden to the team.</li></ul><p>They may not be leading right now, but <strong>their potential can grow if nurtured properly</strong>.</p><hr><h3 id="why-they-should-be-supported-not-left-behind"><strong>Why They Should Be Supported, Not Left Behind</strong></h3><ol><li><strong>Team Continuity Matters More Than Individual Speed</strong><ul><li>A solid team ensures everyone can work <strong>in sync</strong>, not leave others behind.</li><li>In the long run, stability is more valuable than short bursts of performance.</li></ul></li><li><strong>The Domino Effect of a Struggling Member</strong><ul><li>One person feeling left behind can lower <strong>team morale</strong>.</li><li>Faster members may become overburdened trying to cover for slower ones.</li></ul></li><li><strong>Hidden Potential</strong><ul><li>Many “late horses” become <strong>key players</strong> once given <strong>support and training</strong>.</li></ul></li></ol><hr><h3 id="how-to-care-for-the-%E2%80%9Clate-horse%E2%80%9D-physically"><strong>How to Care for the “Late Horse” Physically</strong></h3><ul><li><strong>Intensive Training &amp; Mentoring</strong> – Provide dedicated sessions to strengthen their weak points.</li><li><strong>Balanced Task Distribution</strong> – Assign tasks according to their current capacity, gradually increasing challenges.</li><li><strong>Tools &amp; Resources</strong> – Ensure they have the proper equipment and technology to work efficiently.</li><li><strong>Clear Progress Measurement</strong> – Track growth with simple, visible performance indicators.</li></ul><hr><h3 id="how-to-care-for-the-%E2%80%9Clate-horse%E2%80%9D-emotionally"><strong>How to Care for the “Late Horse” Emotionally</strong></h3><ul><li><strong>Celebrate Small Wins</strong> – Don’t wait for big results; acknowledge daily progress.</li><li><strong>Safe Learning Environment</strong> – Allow them to try without fear of harsh punishment for mistakes.</li><li><strong>Positive Communication</strong> – Avoid demeaning language; use <strong>constructive words</strong> instead.</li><li><strong>Show Trust</strong> – Give realistic but challenging roles so they feel trusted.</li></ul><hr><h3 id="long-term-benefits-of-this-approach"><strong>Long-Term Benefits of This Approach</strong></h3><ul><li><strong>Higher Team Loyalty</strong> – Members feel valued, making them more likely to stay.</li><li><strong>Stable Performance</strong> – Reduced risk of sudden performance drops as all members can meet standards.</li><li><strong>Healthy Work Culture</strong> – Creates an environment where everyone is willing to help each other.</li><li><strong>Individual Transformation</strong> – A “late horse” can turn into a <strong>winning horse</strong> that brings unexpected victories.</li></ul><hr><h3 id="points-to-keep-in-mind"><strong>Points to Keep in Mind</strong></h3><ul><li><strong>Avoid the “Endless Tolerance Zone”</strong><br>There must still be a minimum standard; support should not lead to complacency.</li><li><strong>Balance Your Time Investment</strong><br>Over-focusing on one member can make others feel neglected.</li><li><strong>Set Realistic &amp; Gradual Targets</strong><br>Incremental progress is more sustainable than overwhelming leaps.</li></ul><hr><h3 id="finally"><strong>Finally</strong></h3><p>Winning in a relay—and in the workplace—is not about <strong>discarding the slowest</strong>, but about <strong>strategizing so everyone can run together</strong>. Caring for the “late horse” <strong>physically</strong> means giving them the tools, training, and opportunities to grow. Caring for them <strong>emotionally</strong> means building their confidence, motivation, and sense of belonging.</p><p>In the end, <strong>the winning team is not just fast, but united</strong>.</p><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
