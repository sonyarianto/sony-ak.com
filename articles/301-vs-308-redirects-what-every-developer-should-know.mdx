<p>In the world of web development, <strong>redirects</strong> are one of those things you don’t think about much—until they break something critical. Whether you’re migrating a website, restructuring URLs, or designing APIs, knowing the difference between redirect types can save you from hours of debugging and even lost revenue.</p><p>Two status codes often confused are <strong>301 (Moved Permanently)</strong> and <strong>308 (Permanent Redirect)</strong>. On the surface, they seem almost identical. Both signal that a resource has moved permanently. But dig a little deeper, and the difference can have a big impact on how clients, browsers, and crawlers behave.</p><hr><h2 id="1-the-basics-what-they-do">1. The Basics: What They Do</h2><ul><li><strong>308 (Permanent Redirect)</strong><br>Introduced later (RFC 7538, 2015). It serves the same purpose as 301—<strong>a permanent move</strong>—but it also <strong>guarantees that the request method and body won’t change</strong>.</li></ul><p><strong>301 (Moved Permanently)</strong><br>The oldest and most widely used permanent redirect. It tells clients and search engines:</p><blockquote>“This URL has moved permanently, update your bookmarks and links.”</blockquote><hr><h2 id="2-the-core-difference">2. The Core Difference</h2><p>The key distinction lies in <strong>how the redirect handles HTTP methods</strong>:</p><ul><li><strong>301 Redirect</strong><ul><li>By default, browsers often change the method from <code>POST</code> → <code>GET</code>.</li><li>This means the <strong>request body is lost</strong>.</li><li>Works fine for static content, but dangerous for APIs or form submissions.</li></ul></li><li><strong>308 Redirect</strong><ul><li>Explicitly preserves the <strong>original HTTP method</strong>.</li><li>If a client sends <code>POST</code>, the redirected request will also be a <code>POST</code>—with the same body.</li><li>This makes it safer for APIs and modern web applications.</li></ul></li></ul><p>👉 In short:</p><ul><li><strong>301</strong>: Safe for links, SEO, and static resources.</li><li><strong>308</strong>: Safe for APIs, forms, and cases where you can’t afford to lose the body.</li></ul><hr><h2 id="3-seo-considerations">3. SEO Considerations</h2><p>Search engines (Google, Bing, etc.) treat <strong>301</strong> and <strong>308</strong> the same way:</p><ul><li>Both pass <strong>link equity (“link juice”)</strong>.</li><li>Both signal a <strong>permanent move</strong>.</li><li>Both help consolidate duplicate content under the new URL.</li></ul><p>So from an <strong>SEO perspective</strong>, there’s no downside to using 308 over 301. But since 301 is older and universally recognized, it remains the default choice for most websites.</p><hr><h2 id="4-compatibility-and-support">4. Compatibility and Support</h2><ul><li><strong>301</strong><ul><li>Supported since the early days of HTTP.</li><li>Every client, crawler, and proxy understands it.</li></ul></li><li><strong>308</strong><ul><li>Officially standardized in 2015.</li><li>Fully supported in modern browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge).</li><li>Some very old clients or libraries may not recognize it.</li></ul></li></ul><p><strong>Rule of thumb:</strong> If you’re working on something that must support <strong>legacy clients</strong>, stick with 301. If you’re building a modern API or web app, 308 is often the better choice.</p><hr><h2 id="5-real-world-examples">5. Real-World Examples</h2><ul><li><strong>Website Migration</strong><br>Moving <code>example.com/blog</code> → <code>example.com/articles</code>:<ul><li>A <strong>301 redirect</strong> is perfect. All requests are <code>GET</code> anyway, so no body is lost.</li></ul></li><li><strong>API Endpoint Migration</strong><br>Changing <code>/v1/users</code> → <code>/v2/users</code>:<ul><li>A <strong>308 redirect</strong> is safer. It ensures <code>POST /v1/users</code> with JSON data remains a <code>POST</code> to <code>/v2/users</code> with the same payload.</li></ul></li><li><strong>Form Submission</strong><br>Redirecting after a form submission:<ul><li>Using a 301 could silently drop user input.</li><li>Using a 308 ensures the data stays intact.</li></ul></li></ul><hr><h2 id="6-temporary-counterparts-for-completeness">6. Temporary Counterparts (for completeness)</h2><p>It’s worth mentioning the <strong>temporary versions</strong> as well:</p><ul><li><strong>302 (Found)</strong><br>Old and sometimes ambiguous—clients may switch to <code>GET</code>.</li><li><strong>307 (Temporary Redirect)</strong><br>The safer modern alternative, which <strong>preserves the method and body</strong>.</li></ul><p>👉 Think of <strong>301/308</strong> as the permanent pair, and <strong>302/307</strong> as the temporary pair—with the same method/body distinction.</p><hr><h2 id="7-best-practices-considerations">7. Best Practices &amp; Considerations</h2><ul><li>Use <strong>301</strong> for:<ul><li>SEO redirects</li><li>Website migrations</li><li>Static resource restructuring</li></ul></li><li>Use <strong>308</strong> for:<ul><li>APIs</li><li>Form submissions</li><li>Any case where <strong>request method and body must be preserved</strong></li></ul></li><li><strong>Test before deploying</strong> —especially for APIs. A silent change from <code>POST</code> → <code>GET</code> could mean data loss, duplicate entries, or broken integrations.</li><li><strong>Watch out for caching</strong>. Browsers and proxies cache 301s and 308s aggressively. Once deployed, they’re hard to undo.</li></ul><hr><h2 id="8-finally">8. Finally</h2><p>The difference between <strong>301</strong> and <strong>308</strong> may seem subtle, but in practice, it’s significant.</p><ul><li><strong>301</strong> is the battle-tested workhorse for SEO and website moves.</li><li><strong>308</strong> is the modern, safer choice for APIs and data-sensitive operations.</li></ul><p>As developers, we often learn these things the hard way—when a redirect drops a request body or causes an integration to fail. But knowing the distinction ahead of time lets us choose the right tool for the job.</p><p>👉 <strong>Always remember:</strong></p><ul><li><strong>301 changes methods (POST → GET).</strong></li><li><strong>308 preserves the method and body.</strong></li></ul><p>That one difference can mean the difference between a seamless migration and a production fire.</p><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
