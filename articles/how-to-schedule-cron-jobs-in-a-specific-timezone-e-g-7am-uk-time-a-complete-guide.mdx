<p>When working with scheduled tasks in Linux using <strong>cron jobs</strong>, the default behavior is to follow the <strong>system timezone</strong>. However, when your application or team operates in <strong>multiple timezones</strong> — or you simply want to schedule tasks based on <strong>UK local time</strong> (such as 7 AM London time) — things can become tricky, especially with <strong>Daylight Saving Time (DST)</strong> involved.</p><p>In this article, we’ll walk through how to <strong>reliably run a cron job every day at 7:00 AM UK time</strong>, and cover best practices, common mistakes, and logging techniques to make your cron tasks more manageable and observable.</p><hr><h2 id="%E2%9C%85-the-basics-of-cron">✅ The Basics of Cron</h2><p>A standard cron entry looks like this:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">* * * * * /path/to/command
</code></pre><p>This means:</p><ul><li><code>minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week</code></li><li><code>command</code>: the command you want to run</li></ul><p>So for a daily task at 7 AM, you’d write:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">0 7 * * * /path/to/your-script.sh
</code></pre><p>But this executes at <strong>7 AM based on the server’s timezone</strong>, which may not be UK time.</p><hr><h2 id="%F0%9F%8C%8D-using-timezones-in-cron-with-crontz">🌍 Using Timezones in Cron with <code>CRON_TZ</code></h2><p>The <code>CRON_TZ</code> environment variable allows you to <strong>run a specific cron job in a specific timezone</strong>, even if the server’s timezone is different.</p><p>To run a script at 7:00 AM <strong>UK time</strong>:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">CRON_TZ=Europe/London
0 7 * * * /path/to/your-script.sh
</code></pre><blockquote><strong>✅ Note</strong>: This syntax is supported in <strong>Vixie cron</strong>, the default in most modern Linux distributions like Ubuntu, Debian, and CentOS.</blockquote><hr><h2 id="%F0%9F%A7%A0-why-this-matters-especially-for-uk-time">🧠 Why This Matters (Especially for UK Time)</h2><p>The UK observes <strong>Daylight Saving Time (DST)</strong>:</p><ul><li><strong>GMT (UTC+0)</strong> in winter</li><li><strong>BST (UTC+1)</strong> in summer</li></ul><p>Using <code>CRON_TZ=Europe/London</code> ensures your job runs at <strong>7:00 AM local time</strong>, <strong>automatically adjusting</strong> for DST. Without this, you would have to manually change the schedule or work in UTC.</p><hr><h2 id="%E2%9A%A0%EF%B8%8F-important-notes-gotchas">⚠️ Important Notes &amp; Gotchas</h2><h3 id="1-crontz-only-affects-that-line">1. <strong>CRON_TZ Only Affects That Line</strong></h3><p>Each line in <code>crontab</code> is treated independently. If you want <strong>multiple jobs</strong> to follow the same timezone, each must be prefixed:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">CRON_TZ=Europe/London
0 7 * * * /path/to/first-script.sh

CRON_TZ=Europe/London
30 7 * * * /path/to/second-script.sh
</code></pre><p>There is <strong>no way to set <code>CRON_TZ</code> globally</strong> within a user’s <code>crontab -e</code> file.</p><hr><h3 id="2-log-your-output">2. <strong>Log Your Output</strong></h3><p>By default, cron jobs do <strong>not show any output</strong>. If your script echoes text or encounters an error, it will vanish into the void unless you redirect it:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">CRON_TZ=Europe/London
0 7 * * * /path/to/your-script.sh &gt;&gt; /var/log/my-script.log 2&gt;&amp;1
</code></pre><p>This will:</p><ul><li>Append standard output and errors to <code>/var/log/my-script.log</code></li><li>Help you <strong>debug issues</strong> or verify that the script ran</li></ul><hr><h3 id="3-use-absolute-paths">3. <strong>Use Absolute Paths</strong></h3><p>Cron jobs <strong>do not run in your usual environment</strong>. Always use <strong>full paths</strong> for everything:</p><p>✅ Good:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">/usr/bin/php /home/user/myapp/script.php
</code></pre><p>❌ Bad:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">php script.php
</code></pre><p>To discover the correct path, use:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">which php
which node
which bash
</code></pre><hr><h3 id="4-check-if-cron-is-running">4. <strong>Check if Cron is Running</strong></h3><p>Your job won’t execute if the cron service is not active.</p><p>On RHEL/CentOS:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">sudo systemctl status crond
</code></pre><p>On Ubuntu/Debian:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">sudo systemctl status cron
</code></pre><p>Ensure it's <strong>enabled and running</strong>, especially after a reboot.</p><hr><h3 id="5-how-to-verify-your-job-ran">5. <strong>How to Verify Your Job Ran</strong></h3><p>Here are a few ways to confirm execution:</p><ul><li>Check your <strong>log file</strong> (if you redirected output).</li></ul><p>Add a timestamp:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">echo "Job ran at $(date)" &gt;&gt; /var/log/my-script.log
</code></pre><p>Include a <code>logger</code> command inside your script:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">logger "My 7AM UK cron job ran"
</code></pre><p>This will write to <code>/var/log/syslog</code> or <code>/var/log/messages</code>.</p><hr><h2 id="%F0%9F%A7%AA-test-before-going-live">🧪 Test Before Going Live</h2><p>Want to be sure everything works before committing to a long-term schedule?</p><p>Set your job to run a few minutes from now for testing:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">CRON_TZ=Europe/London
15 14 * * * /path/to/your-script.sh &gt;&gt; /tmp/test.log 2&gt;&amp;1
</code></pre><p>If it works, you can change it back to:</p><pre><code class="language-bash">CRON_TZ=Europe/London
0 7 * * * /path/to/your-script.sh
</code></pre><hr><h2 id="%F0%9F%9B%A1%EF%B8%8F-best-practices-recap">🛡️ Best Practices Recap</h2><ul><li>✅ <strong>Use <code>CRON_TZ</code></strong> to schedule timezone-specific jobs reliably</li><li>✅ <strong>Log everything</strong> — especially during development and testing</li><li>✅ <strong>Use absolute paths</strong> to avoid environment-related failures</li><li>✅ <strong>Confirm cron is running</strong> on the server</li><li>✅ <strong>Test on a short interval</strong> before setting long-term schedules</li><li>✅ <strong>Monitor for failures</strong> using logs or external alerting</li></ul><hr><h2 id="%F0%9F%A7%AD-final-thoughts">🧭 Final Thoughts</h2><p>Handling timezones in cron jobs doesn’t have to be a pain — as long as you use <code>CRON_TZ</code> correctly and stick to <strong>logging and verification practices</strong>. This is especially important for time-sensitive workflows like sending scheduled emails, generating reports, or triggering time-based jobs for users in <strong>specific regions like the UK</strong>.</p><p>With the right setup, your cron job will run <strong>daily at exactly 7:00 AM local UK time</strong>, regardless of whether the UK is on GMT or BST — and without needing manual intervention when clocks change.</p><p>Stay sharp, and happy scheduling! 🕰️</p><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
