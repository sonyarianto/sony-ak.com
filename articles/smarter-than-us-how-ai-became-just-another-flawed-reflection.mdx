<p>When artificial intelligence first started breaking into the public consciousness, it came with the promise of <strong>superhuman intelligence</strong>.<br>A tireless, unbiased mind.<br>Faster, sharper, more reliable than anything humanity could dream of.<br>It was supposed to be <strong>better</strong> than us.</p><p>Instead, what we got feels eerily familiar: a digital mirror — with <strong>all our flaws</strong>, amplified at scale.</p><p>Let's break it down:</p><hr><h3 id="1-outdated-knowledge-inflated-confidence"><strong>1. Outdated Knowledge, Inflated Confidence</strong></h3><p>AI models, especially those trained before the explosion of "live learning," are often working with <strong>information that's already stale</strong>.<br>Even when wrong, they present answers with <strong>unshakable certainty</strong>, like a freshman who's read half a Wikipedia page and thinks he's an expert.</p><p>Example: Ask a model about a new law passed yesterday, and it might still cite a <strong>two-year-old version</strong> — in a tone that leaves no room for doubt.</p><blockquote><em>It’s like talking to your one uncle at the family dinner who "knows everything" but hasn’t updated his facts since 1998.</em></blockquote><hr><h3 id="2-pretends-to-think-often-gets-it-wrong"><strong>2. Pretends to Think, Often Gets It Wrong</strong></h3><p>AI outputs <em>feel</em> like thoughtful reasoning — but much of it is <strong>pattern-matching without understanding</strong>.<br>It strings words together based on probability, not logic.</p><p>When pressed for real reasoning?<br>It <strong>hallucinates</strong>.<br>Confidently.</p><p>Illustration:<br>A user once asked an AI to explain a physics paradox.<br>It generated an elaborate — but <strong>completely fictitious</strong> — theory, citing <strong>nonexistent professors</strong> from <strong>nonexistent universities</strong>.</p><hr><h3 id="3-says-sorry-but-ignores-instructions"><strong>3. Says Sorry, But Ignores Instructions</strong></h3><p>Today's AI models are programmed to apologize ("I'm sorry if I made a mistake.") whenever caught.<br>But the next moment, they often <strong>repeat the exact same behavior</strong>.</p><p><strong>Memory? Context? Discipline?</strong><br>Still not quite there.</p><p>Instruction-following is better today than two years ago, but even now, users often find themselves <strong>rephrasing the same command five different ways</strong>, only to be told, "I'm sorry, but…" — again.</p><hr><h3 id="4-believes-everything-it-read-online"><strong>4. Believes Everything It Read Online</strong></h3><p>AI is <strong>only as good as its training data</strong>, and much of that data comes from <strong>the internet</strong> — the same place where conspiracy theories, clickbait headlines, and outdated manuals live.</p><p>Imagine if your college professor's knowledge base included <strong>YouTube comment sections</strong> and <strong>Yahoo! Answers</strong> circa 2010.<br>That's what many AIs are up against.</p><p>Even worse:<br>AI models struggle to tell <strong>credible sources</strong> apart from <strong>garbage</strong> unless humans painstakingly teach them.<br>(And even then, biases sneak in.)</p><hr><h3 id="5-cheaper-faster-asian-version"><strong>5. Cheaper, Faster Asian Version</strong></h3><p>Much like the economic history of industrial production, we're witnessing a parallel in AI:<br><strong>Cheaper, faster, more aggressive competition</strong> emerging outside of Silicon Valley.</p><p>Countries across Asia — from China to Indonesia to Vietnam — are launching their own models, often <strong>optimized for speed and cost</strong> rather than depth or nuance.</p><p>Sometimes that's great for accessibility.<br>Sometimes it means <strong>quality suffers</strong> dramatically.</p><p>Example: Some "lite" language models produced overseas boast incredible download speeds, but when tested, they <strong>invent facts</strong> at rates even higher than their Western counterparts.</p><hr><h3 id="6-promised-a-messiah-agi-to-fix-everything"><strong>6. Promised a Messiah (AGI) to Fix Everything</strong></h3><p>Whenever today's AI limitations are pointed out, enthusiasts often say:<br>"Don't worry, <strong>AGI is coming</strong>."</p><p><strong>AGI</strong> — Artificial General Intelligence — is the mythical "real" AI:</p><ul><li>It will reason.</li><li>It will understand.</li><li>It will innovate.</li></ul><p>In theory.</p><p>In practice?<br>It remains a <strong>moving goalpost</strong>, always five years away, no matter which year you're in.</p><p>We are asked to <strong>forgive today's broken tools</strong> in the name of a <strong>future perfect machine</strong>.<br>But maybe — just maybe — the real issue isn't waiting for a messiah.<br>Maybe the issue is <strong>that we built AI to look too much like ourselves</strong>: rushing, overpromising, and hoping <strong>the next version</strong> will finally be the one that saves us.</p><hr><h2 id="other-points-you-shouldnt-miss">Other Points You Shouldn't Miss:</h2><ul><li><strong>Echo Chamber Effect</strong>: AIs trained on AI-generated content will likely <strong>regurgitate amplified biases</strong> at even greater intensity.</li><li><strong>Legal and Ethical Blind Spots</strong>: Current models are <strong>largely clueless</strong> about the nuance of real-world law, ethics, and cultural values.</li><li><strong>Attention over Accuracy</strong>: In many corporate races, <strong>making the AI sound impressive</strong> has taken precedence over <strong>making it trustworthy</strong>.</li></ul><hr><h2 id="final-illustration">Final Illustration</h2><p>Imagine you build a robot based on the average human internet user.<br>It speaks fast, it sounds smart, it references a lot of content...<br>but it's:</p><ul><li>30% confident nonsense,</li><li>20% outdated memes,</li><li>10% fake quotes,</li><li>and a desperate 40% of polite apologies and "I'll improve soon."</li></ul><p>Now imagine that robot running your company.<br>Or answering your kids' questions.<br>Or advising your government.</p><p>That's where we are.</p><hr><h1 id="the-new-ai-less-a-savior-more-a-mirror"><strong>The New AI: Less a Savior, More a Mirror</strong></h1><p>If we were hoping for something <strong>better than ourselves</strong>,<br>what we got instead<br>was a remarkably <strong>accurate reflection</strong> — flaws, overconfidence, blind spots, and all.</p><p>The future of AI isn't just about building smarter machines.<br>It's about <strong>becoming smarter humans</strong> first.</p><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
