<p>In recent months, many young founders have tried <strong>pitching startups</strong> to investors. Some have even built <strong>AI Agents</strong> or other tools packaged as SaaS (Software as a Service).</p><p>The reality, however, is harsh: <strong>a SaaS business focused on low-GDP countries is often valued at $0 by global investors.</strong> Why?</p><hr><h2 id="1-gdp-and-purchasing-power">1. GDP and Purchasing Power</h2><p><strong>SaaS only makes sense when services are expensive.</strong></p><ul><li>In countries with low GDP, <strong>labor costs are cheaper than software subscriptions</strong>.</li><li>Example: if an employee’s monthly salary is only $250–300, companies would rather hire an extra admin than pay $100/month for SaaS.</li><li>Investor research shows that SaaS thrives when <strong>GDP per capita exceeds $8,000</strong> — that’s when companies start realizing it’s cheaper to pay for software than for additional staff.</li></ul><p>Indonesia’s GDP per capita is currently around <strong>$4,960</strong>, which is far below that threshold.</p><hr><h2 id="2-saas-friendly-countries-in-southeast-asia">2. SaaS-Friendly Countries in Southeast Asia</h2><p>Based on economic data, only a few countries in the region have <strong>enough purchasing power to support SaaS growth</strong>:</p><ul><li><strong>Singapore</strong></li><li><strong>Brunei</strong></li><li><strong>Malaysia</strong></li></ul><p>Other countries — Indonesia, Vietnam, the Philippines, Thailand — struggle unless the SaaS is <strong>ultra-cheap</strong> or solves a <strong>critical pain point</strong> that cannot be addressed manually.</p><hr><h2 id="3-implications-for-founders">3. Implications for Founders</h2><p>If you’re building SaaS in a country with lower GDP, here are some practical strategies:</p><ul><li><strong>Build locally, sell globally</strong><br>Keep your development team in a lower-cost country, but sell to high-GDP markets (US, Europe, Japan).</li><li><strong>Freemium + Ads / Upsell</strong><br>Because <strong>willingness to pay is low</strong>, focus on large user bases, then monetize through ads or premium features.</li><li><strong>Hybrid: SaaS + Service</strong><br>Many local SaaS businesses succeed when combined with a <strong>service layer</strong> (e.g., an agency that builds its own SaaS and sells it bundled with consulting).</li><li><strong>Vertical SaaS for high-margin sectors</strong><br>Target industries or consumer groups with high margins (oil &amp; gas, finance, healthcare, premium B2B).</li></ul><hr><h2 id="4-why-investors-see-%E2%80%9Czero-value%E2%80%9D-in-local-saas">4. Why Investors See “Zero Value” in Local SaaS</h2><p>Investors do not invest in a name, a brand, or even AI hype. They focus on:</p><ul><li><strong>Market size</strong> — how many paying customers exist in this country?</li><li><strong>Revenue potential</strong> — how much can you realistically charge?</li><li><strong>Scalability</strong> — can this product expand beyond a low-GDP region?</li></ul><p>If the answer is “we’re targeting only the local market,” investors often conclude: <strong>value = 0</strong>.</p><hr><h2 id="5-conclusion">5. Conclusion</h2><p>This doesn’t mean SaaS cannot exist in low-GDP countries. But it does mean that <strong>pure-play B2B SaaS targeting the local market has almost no chance of becoming a unicorn.</strong></p><p>Models that stand a better chance include:</p><ul><li><strong>Consumer apps with massive volume (freemium + ads)</strong></li><li><strong>Service companies with SaaS as a backend (agency + software)</strong></li><li><strong>Global SaaS built locally, but sold internationally</strong></li></ul><hr><p>👉 The core issue is not technology or ideas. It’s <strong>purchasing power</strong>. As long as GDP per capita remains low, it will always be <strong>cheaper to hire humans than to pay for SaaS</strong> — and investors know this well.</p><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
