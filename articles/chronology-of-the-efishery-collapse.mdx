<p>Based on this epic article.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-bookmark-card"><a class="kg-bookmark-container" href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-04-15/how-indonesian-startup-efishery-s-ex-ceo-gibran-huzaifah-faked-the-numbers"><div class="kg-bookmark-content"><div class="kg-bookmark-title">CEO Explains How He Faked Results in $300 Million Meltdown</div><div class="kg-bookmark-description">Agritech venture eFishery was one of Asia’s brightest startups with money from the likes of SoftBank and Temasek. Then it all came crashing down.</div><div class="kg-bookmark-metadata"><img class="kg-bookmark-icon" src="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/icon/favicon-black.png" alt=""><span class="kg-bookmark-author">Bloomberg</span><span class="kg-bookmark-publisher">David Ramli</span></div></div><div class="kg-bookmark-thumbnail"><img src="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/thumbnail/1200x800.jpg" alt="" onerror="this.style.display = 'none'"></div></a></figure><h3 id="2012"><strong>2012</strong></h3><ul><li>Gibran Huzaifah graduates from ITB and dreams of being <em>“Raja Lele”</em> with <strong>1,000</strong> ponds.</li><li>He starts building fish-feeding technology to solve feeding inefficiencies.</li></ul><h3 id="2013"><strong>2013</strong></h3><ul><li>Gibran begins pitching the idea of automated fish feeders at startup competitions.</li><li>A key pitch video from October 2013 shows him at a floating fish farm.</li></ul><h3 id="2015"><strong>2015</strong></h3><ul><li>Dutch aquaculture fund Aqua-Spark joins a <strong>$750,000</strong> seed round investment.</li></ul><h3 id="late-2017"><strong>Late 2017</strong></h3><ul><li>eFishery faces serious cash problems: only <strong>$8,142</strong> in the bank.</li></ul><h3 id="may-2018"><strong>May 2018</strong></h3><ul><li>Aqua-Spark agrees to invest <strong>$1.5M</strong> in 3 tranches for Series A. The last <strong>$500K</strong> is conditional on attracting other investors.</li></ul><h3 id="late-2018"><strong>Late 2018</strong></h3><ul><li>Gibran fabricates numbers in Excel to make eFishery look successful, triggering his first major deception.</li><li>Investors are convinced and invest more capital — the beginning of dual accounting books.</li></ul><h3 id="2019"><strong>2019</strong></h3><ul><li>Revenue reportedly jumps from <strong>$185K</strong> (2018) to <strong>$10M</strong> (2019) due to platform manipulation.</li><li>eFishery becomes gross-profitable on paper.</li><li>Expansion accelerates, attracting even more investor interest.</li></ul><h3 id="2020"><strong>2020</strong></h3><ul><li>Series B raises <strong>$20M</strong>, co-led by Northstar Group and Go-Ventures (now Argor Capital).</li><li>Global ESG investment trends make eFishery attractive to socially-conscious funds.</li><li>Real financial performance diverges further from internal reality.</li></ul><h3 id="2021"><strong>2021</strong></h3><ul><li>Gibran pitches SoftBank founder Masayoshi Son and receives a term sheet valuing eFishery at<strong> $200M</strong>.</li><li>Competing offers from Sequoia (Peak XV) and Temasek push valuation over $300M.</li><li>Eventually, <strong>$90M</strong> is raised at a <strong>$410M</strong> valuation from major funds.</li><li>Actual financials: revenue <strong>~40%</strong> lower than reported; pre-tax loss instead of profit.</li><li>Gibran remains conflicted but does not stop accepting new funds.</li></ul><h3 id="early-2022"><strong>Early 2022</strong></h3><ul><li>A staff member proposes a shell company network and transaction padding scheme.</li><li>eFishery expands the manipulation to include over <strong>5,000</strong> fake accounts across subsidiaries.</li><li>Default rates in lending platform rise; real cash increasingly used to fuel fake growth.</li></ul><h3 id="2023"><strong>2023</strong></h3><ul><li>Series D raises <strong>$200M</strong> at <strong>$1.4B</strong> valuation led by Abu Dhabi’s 42XFund.</li><li>eFishery expands to India and reports rapid profitability.</li><li>Internal concerns rise; supply chain and market players begin to question inflated claims.</li></ul><h3 id="late-november-2024"><strong>Late November 2024</strong></h3><ul><li>Whistleblower complaint is sent to a board member highlighting major discrepancies.</li></ul><h3 id="dec-6-2024"><strong>Dec 6, 2024</strong></h3><ul><li>Gibran is confronted by the board regarding internal inconsistencies and whistleblower evidence.</li></ul><h3 id="dec-9-2024"><strong>Dec 9, 2024</strong></h3><ul><li>Gibran confesses to eFishery's leadership team during a meeting.</li></ul><h3 id="dec-11-2024"><strong>Dec 11, 2024</strong></h3><ul><li>Gibran comes clean in a Zoom call with early investor Amy Novogratz (Aqua-Spark).</li></ul><h3 id="dec-13-2024"><strong>Dec 13, 2024</strong></h3><ul><li>Gibran is suspended by the board. Interim CEO and CFO are appointed. His access is revoked.</li></ul><h3 id="early-2025"><strong>Early 2025</strong></h3><ul><li>FTI Consulting is hired to manage the company and assess viability.</li><li>Machines are scrapped for <strong>~$6</strong> apiece.</li><li>42XFund’s <strong>$100M</strong> may only return <strong>$8.3M</strong>.</li><li>Most employees laid off; operations ceased.</li></ul><hr><h2 id="%F0%9F%93%8C-summary-and-lessons">📌 Summary and Lessons</h2><ul><li><strong>The fraud began with desperation</strong>, when Gibran, facing cash depletion in 2018, altered financials to save the company.</li><li>Over time, <strong>this evolved into systemic manipulation</strong> involving fake shell companies, duplicate books, padded revenue, and fictitious user data.</li><li><strong>Top-tier investors</strong>, including SoftBank, Temasek, Peak XV, and 42XFund, were lured in by the appearance of exponential growth and impact.</li><li>Gibran justified his actions using <strong>utilitarian logic</strong>, often referring to the <strong>trolley problem</strong>: sacrificing truth for perceived greater good.</li><li>However, <strong>his impact narrative masked fundamental unsustainability</strong>—loan defaults were high, real margin was extremely thin, and tech adoption was far lower than reported.</li><li><strong>Due diligence failures</strong> and investor FOMO played critical roles in enabling the deception.</li><li>In the end, <strong>over $300 million was lost</strong>, and thousands of farmers like Suganda were left with broken machines and shrinking incomes.</li><li>Gibran didn’t flee or visibly enrich himself but now lives a modest life and claims the money went into operations and growth, not personal gain.</li></ul><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
