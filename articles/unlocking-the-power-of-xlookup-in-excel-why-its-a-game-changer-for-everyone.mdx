<script async="" src="https://platform.twitter.com/widgets.js" charset="utf-8"></script></figure><p>If you've been working with Excel for a while, chances are you’ve struggled with <strong>VLOOKUP</strong>, <strong>HLOOKUP</strong>, or the often clumsy combination of <strong>INDEX</strong> and <strong>MATCH</strong>.<br>While they served us well, <strong>Excel’s XLOOKUP function</strong> arrived like a breath of fresh air — solving so many old pains elegantly.</p><p>In this article, we’ll dive into <strong>what XLOOKUP is</strong>, <strong>why it’s better</strong>, <strong>how to use it</strong>, and <strong>some real-world tricks</strong> you should know to level up your spreadsheets.</p><hr><h2 id="what-is-xlookup">What is XLOOKUP?</h2><p>In simple terms, <strong>XLOOKUP is the modern way to search for something in Excel</strong>.<br>Instead of looking only from <strong>left to right</strong> (like <code>VLOOKUP</code>) or getting tangled with column numbers, <strong>XLOOKUP</strong> lets you <strong>find a value anywhere</strong> and <strong>return a matching value cleanly</strong>.</p><p>The basic structure is extremely readable:</p><pre><code class="language-excel">XLOOKUP(lookup_value, lookup_array, return_array, [if_not_found], [match_mode], [search_mode])
</code></pre><p><strong>That's it.</strong> No messy column numbers. No hidden approximate matches. No fear of breaking your formulas when inserting new columns.</p><hr><h2 id="why-xlookup-is-better-than-vlookup">Why XLOOKUP is Better Than VLOOKUP</h2><p>Here’s a quick side-by-side you need to remember:</p>

<p>You want to find Jessica’s salary by entering her ID.</p><p>With XLOOKUP, the formula is incredibly intuitive:</p><pre><code class="language-excel">=XLOOKUP(53, B2:B6, D2:D6)
</code></pre><p>No more worrying whether you should put “column 3” or “column 4” — <strong>you directly tell Excel where to search and where to fetch</strong>.</p><hr><h2 id="handling-errors-gracefully">Handling Errors Gracefully</h2><p>One brilliant feature: <strong>built-in error handling</strong>.<br>If a value is missing, you can instruct XLOOKUP to return a custom message:</p><pre><code class="language-excel">=XLOOKUP(999, B2:B6, D2:D6, "Not Found")
</code></pre><p>Instead of throwing ugly <code>#N/A</code> errors at your user, you can now <strong>design a much more user-friendly spreadsheet</strong>.</p><hr><h2 id="beyond-basics-powerful-options">Beyond Basics: Powerful Options</h2><p>XLOOKUP also allows <strong>wildcard matching</strong> — handy for fuzzy searches:</p><pre><code class="language-excel">=XLOOKUP("*mia*", C2:C6, D2:D6, "Not Found", 2)
</code></pre><p>And if you want <strong>to search from the bottom up</strong> (not just top to bottom), you can set <code>search_mode</code> to <code>-1</code>.<br>This is <strong>extremely useful</strong> if your data contains multiple entries and you want the latest one.</p><p>Example:</p><pre><code class="language-excel">=XLOOKUP(30, B2:B6, D2:D6, "Not Found", 0, -1)
</code></pre><hr><h2 id="important-considerations-before-you-switch">Important Considerations Before You Switch</h2><ul><li><strong>Version Compatibility:</strong><br>XLOOKUP is <strong>only available in Excel 365, Excel 2019, and later versions</strong>.<br>If you’re sharing files with people using Excel 2016 or older, they won’t see XLOOKUP — they'll see broken formulas.</li><li><strong>Performance on Massive Datasets:</strong><br>XLOOKUP is efficient, but if you are handling <strong>hundreds of thousands of rows</strong>, it still pays to be careful with how you structure lookups (e.g., avoid recalculating unnecessarily).</li><li><strong>Array Returns:</strong><br>One little-known but powerful feature: <strong>XLOOKUP can return multiple columns at once</strong>.<br>You are not limited to returning just one field!</li></ul><p>Example:</p><pre><code class="language-excel">=XLOOKUP(53, B2:B6, C2:D6)
</code></pre><p>returns <strong>both Name and Salary</strong> at once!</p><hr><h2 id="finally">Finally</h2><p>If you are still building everything with <strong>VLOOKUP</strong>, it is time to <strong>modernize your Excel skills</strong>.<br><strong>XLOOKUP is simpler, stronger, and smarter</strong> — it embodies what good software evolution looks like: fewer rules, fewer mistakes, better results.</p><p>It is not an exaggeration to say:<br><strong>Once you start using XLOOKUP, you will never want to go back.</strong></p><h3 id="support-us">Support Us</h3>
